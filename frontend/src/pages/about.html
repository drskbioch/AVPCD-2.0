About

AVPCD is a curated collection of antiviral potency values (IC50, EC50, IC90, EC90 and ED50) of compounds from plants. Every record ties one value to its compound, plant, virus, assay and the paper it was read from.

What the records are

Each record is one potency value of one plant-derived compound against one virus in one assay, copied from one paper. The database holds – records from – papers. They are the core of a larger curated set of 1,988 records: reference drugs, single-concentration % inhibition rows, semisynthetic derivatives, plant-virus assays and one confirmed duplicate are left out.

Where a value was read

Every record says where its value was read. Full text means the value was read in the full paper (– records). Abstract means it was read from the abstract only (–). Unclear (–) has not been ruled on yet. Abstract values are less reliable than full-text values, so filter on this when it matters.

How the records were made

Four curators extracted the records from the papers assigned to them, with AI-tool assistance, and reviewed them. Papers were screened by title and abstract. This description comes from the curators and has not been independently verified. The outputs of the AI tools were not kept, so the extraction route cannot be rebuilt from the record.

Added by AVPCD, not by the curators: the evidence tier, structures (InChIKey and SMILES), plant and virus taxonomy, nanomolar values, the comparison with NPASS and ChEMBL, the plant-provenance level and the data quality flags.

Known limits

  • A unit may be wrong on three papers. The recorded unit may be 1,000 times away from the unit in the paper (recorded mg/mL or mM; the paper may say ug/mL or uM). Ten potency values lie on these papers. They carry the flag “Unit to check” and should be compared with the paper before use.
  • Compound identity is a name lookup. About two thirds of the records have a structure, and no match was checked by hand. Matches that were only approximate were rejected.
  • Plant. Some records have no plant species. For many of them a plant was found only for the compound in an external database, and that is not the plant of the assay sample. The record says which case applies.
  • Novelty is a range, not a number. Each record is compared with NPASS and ChEMBL by structure, virus species and value (within 10%). The number of newly curated records is a lower bound; records that cannot be tested (no structure, no virus species, no comparable value) are counted apart. ChEMBL misses assays whose target is a host cell.
  • Open rulings. One record has an unclear derivative status, and for 47 records it has not been ruled whether the value was read from the full text or from the abstract.
  • Data quality flags mark possible problems: a possible duplicate, a surrogate system without its tag, a value outside 1 pM to 1 mM or without a molecular weight, an unconfirmed source tier, a unit to check. A flag does not mean a value is wrong, and a record without a flag has not been checked either.

What is not here

No NPASS or ChEMBL values (only their identifiers), no abstract or full text of any paper, no curator identifiers, and no record outside the core set.
